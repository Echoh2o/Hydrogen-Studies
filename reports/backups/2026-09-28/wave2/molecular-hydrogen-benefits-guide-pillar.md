**Short answer:** The best-supported benefit of hydrogen water is a small one. In pooled trials of healthy adults, molecular hydrogen made exercise feel slightly easier and lowered blood lactate, but it didn't improve endurance, strength or VO2max [1]. Hydrogen-rich water also lowered total and LDL cholesterol by a few points in adults with overweight or obesity, which the reviewers said is too small to matter for heart risk [2]. Claims about sleep, mood, skin, brain health and aging rest on a few small trials or on animal studies. The largest trials in patients, with 178 people with Parkinson's disease and 675 with COVID-19, found no benefit over placebo [3][4][5]. Below, we grade each claimed benefit by the strength of its human evidence.

## What is molecular hydrogen?

Molecular hydrogen (H₂) is a gas made of two hydrogen atoms bonded together. Hydrogen water is ordinary drinking water with a small amount of this gas dissolved in it. It can also be inhaled or infused as hydrogen-rich saline; in clinical trials, inhalation is the most common route, followed by drinking water [6]. Our [hydrogen water explainer](/blog/what-is-hydrogen-water) and [ppm guide](/blog/hydrogen-water-ppm-levels) cover how it's made and measured.

**What does hydrogen do in the body?** Nobody knows for sure. A 2007 study from Nippon Medical School in Japan reported that H₂ neutralized hydroxyl radicals, a highly damaging form of oxygen, in cultured cells, and that inhaling it reduced brain injury in rats after an induced stroke [7]. That "selective antioxidant" idea launched the field, but later chemistry work challenged it: hydrogen reacts with hydroxyl radicals too slowly to matter in the body, and it didn't react at all with peroxynitrous acid, another oxidant it was thought to neutralize [8]. The mechanism is still debated.

**How much research exists?** Our database lists more than 2,200 papers on molecular hydrogen. About half are animal studies, and fewer than 1 in 5 are studies in people [9]. A 2023 review counted 81 registered clinical trials and 64 published human studies across all delivery methods [6].

## What are the benefits of hydrogen water, according to the evidence?

Our grades follow the logic of GRADE, the system medical reviewers use: start from the study design, then grade down for small samples, inconsistent results, short follow-up and outcomes that don't directly measure health.

| Lower cholesterol | Human RCTs, pooled | 13 RCTs, 757 adults with overweight or obesity [2] | Total cholesterol about 6.7 mg/dL lower, LDL about 3.2 mg/dL lower; triglycerides unchanged | Moderate (small effect) |

## Does hydrogen water reduce exercise fatigue?

Probably a little. A 2024 meta-analysis pooled 27 publications with 597 healthy adults who drank hydrogen water, bathed in it, inhaled hydrogen gas or swallowed a hydrogen-releasing powder [1]. Compared with placebo, hydrogen slightly lowered how hard exercise felt and blood lactate. It didn't improve VO2max, endurance or muscle strength; the one performance gain was small, in jumping power [1].

So the effect is on how tired people felt and on one blood marker, not on how far or fast they could go. Individual trials are in our [exercise recovery hub](/explore-by-condition/exercise-recovery).

## Does hydrogen water lower cholesterol or blood sugar?

**Cholesterol: real but small.** A 2026 meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials in 757 adults with overweight or obesity found total cholesterol about 6.7 mg/dL lower and LDL about 3.2 mg/dL lower with hydrogen-rich water. HDL ("good") cholesterol fell slightly, and triglycerides didn't change. The authors said these effects are below the size that matters for heart-disease risk and don't justify using hydrogen water to lower cholesterol outside research [2]. See the [cholesterol hub](/explore-by-condition/cholesterol).

**Blood sugar: mixed.** In a 24-week trial, 60 adults with metabolic syndrome drank water made with hydrogen-producing tablets (more than 5.5 millimoles of H₂ a day) and ended up with lower cholesterol, glucose and HbA1c than the placebo group [10]. But in 50 people with type 2 diabetes, electrolyzed hydrogen water didn't change insulin resistance, the main outcome, compared with filtered water [11]. Two trials pointing different ways don't add up to a reliable effect. More studies are in the [metabolic syndrome hub](/explore-by-condition/metabolic-syndrome).

**Liver.** A 2024 meta-analysis of 8 randomized trials (433 people with various liver problems) reported slight decreases in the liver enzymes ALT, AST and ALP [12]. In an 8-week trial of 30 people with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, liver enzymes didn't change significantly [13]. See the [fatty liver hub](/explore-by-condition/fatty-liver-nafld).

## Is hydrogen water an antioxidant in people?

This is the most-marketed benefit and one of the least consistent in human trials.

- In 38 healthy adults who drank 1.5 liters a day for 4 weeks, the main blood markers of oxidative stress didn't differ from plain water, though the hydrogen group showed less immune-cell death and lower activity in inflammation-related genes [15].
- In 59 adults with chronic mountain sickness who drank about 1 liter a day for 8 weeks, hydrogen water raised total antioxidant capacity but also raised malondialdehyde, a marker of oxidative damage. The authors concluded it didn't reduce oxidative damage and might increase it in people with a higher body mass index [16].
- A meta-analysis of 6 exercise studies (76 people) found no drop in a standard oxidative-stress marker, though a measure of antioxidant capacity rose slightly [14].

An antioxidant effect in a lab dish doesn't guarantee one in a person. See the [oxidative stress](/explore-by-condition/oxidative-stress) and [inflammation](/explore-by-condition/inflammation) hubs.

## Can hydrogen water help with sleep, mood or stress?

The evidence is thin.

- **Mood.** In a 4-week crossover trial, 26 adults drank 600 mL a day of hydrogen water or the same mineral water without hydrogen. Hydrogen water lowered a mood-and-anxiety questionnaire score (the K6) and resting sympathetic nerve activity more than placebo [17].
- **Inhaled gas.** In a single-blind trial, 66 people with sleep problems inhaled a hydrogen-oxygen mixture or air for 1 hour twice a day for 7 days. The hydrogen group slept longer and scored better on sleep-quality and depression questionnaires; anxiety scores didn't differ [18].
- **Drinking water.** In an 8-week trial of 36 adults with obesity, 1 liter a day of hydrogen-rich water improved self-rated sleep quality and food cravings, but both results sat at the edge of statistical significance (p = 0.05) [19].

That's "worth studying," not "proven." Related studies are in the [anxiety and stress hub](/explore-by-condition/anxiety-stress).

## What about brain health, skin and aging?

**Memory and thinking.** In a one-year trial, 73 people with mild cognitive impairment drank about 300 mL a day of hydrogen water or placebo water. Cognitive scores didn't differ overall; a benefit appeared only in carriers of the APOE4 gene variant, a lead for future research rather than a result [20]. In a 6-month trial of 40 adults aged 70 and older, cognitive function also didn't differ from control [21]. See the [cognitive function hub](/explore-by-condition/cognitive-function).

**Aging markers.** In that 6-month trial, telomeres (protective caps on chromosomes) lengthened slightly with hydrogen water and shortened with control water, and chair-stand performance improved. Most other measures, including sleep, quality of life and facial skin features, didn't differ [21]. One lab marker in 40 people isn't evidence of slower aging.

**Skin.** The main human signal is a pilot trial in 21 people awaiting keloid scar surgery: those who drank hydrogen water reported less pain and itching and had lower scar-appearance scores than those who drank hydrogen-free water [22]. That's an early signal in one scar condition, not evidence about wrinkles or everyday skin. See the [skin aging hub](/explore-by-condition/skin-aging).

## Does molecular hydrogen therapy treat any disease?

No disease treatment has been established. "Molecular hydrogen therapy" usually means breathing hydrogen gas or drinking hydrogen water as a treatment, and a 2023 review found positive signals in human studies of heart, lung, brain and cancer conditions [6]. When trials got bigger, though, benefits often didn't hold up:

- **Parkinson's disease.** After an earlier 48-week trial looked promising, the same team enrolled 178 people at 14 hospitals to drink 1 liter of hydrogen water or placebo water a day. After 72 weeks, symptom scores didn't differ [3], and a later inhaled-gas pilot by the same group, in 20 people, also showed no benefit [4].
- **COVID-19.** In a phase 3 trial, 675 outpatients with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 drank hydrogen-rich water or placebo twice a day for 21 days. Worsening by day 14 was similar: 46.1% with hydrogen, 43.5% with placebo [5].
- **Infant epilepsy.** In 53 infants with epileptic spasms, adding inhaled hydrogen-oxygen gas to standard treatment for 14 days didn't improve spasm control [23].

Hydrogen water isn't a substitute for medical care. If you have a health condition, talk with your clinician before adding it.

## Is hydrogen water safe?

Our [side effects guide](/blog/hydrogen-water-side-effects) covers what human trials report. In the trials above, adverse events were about as common with hydrogen water as with placebo in the COVID-19 trial (27% vs 26.2%) [5], but more common in the mountain-sickness trial (50% vs 37%, mostly dizziness) [16]. In infants, inhaled hydrogen-oxygen gas was linked to more raised heart-enzyme results (28.3% vs 10.9%), digestive problems and abnormal liver tests than inhaled air [23]. For amounts used in trials, see [how much hydrogen water per day](/blog/how-much-hydrogen-water-per-day).

## What the evidence doesn't show

- **Small samples.** Many cholesterol trials had fewer than 50 people per group [2]; five exercise studies had 10 or fewer participants [1].
- **Short durations.** Most cholesterol trials lasted 8–12 weeks [2]. The longest trials here, at one year and 72 weeks, found no benefit on their main outcomes [4][20].
- **Stand-in outcomes.** Most trials measured blood markers, questionnaires or fitness tests, not heart attacks, diagnoses or lifespan.
- **Doses all over the map.** In the exercise studies, hydrogen water ranged from 0.5 to 5.9 ppm and inhaled gas from 1% to 68% hydrogen [1]; daily doses in the cholesterol trials ranged from under 3 mg to over 12 mg [2]. With no standard dose, "hydrogen water works" isn't one testable claim.
- **Industry involvement.** The metabolic syndrome trial used a hydrogen-tablet company's tablets [10]; the obesity trial's drink came from a company one author is affiliated with [19]; two authors of the inhaled-gas sleep trial were affiliated with the device maker [18]. Ties don't make a result wrong, but they raise the bar for replication. Our funder sells hydrogen products, so apply the same skepticism to us.
- **Publication bias.** Meta-analyses found no statistical sign of it, but with few studies those tests are weak, and selective reporting can't be ruled out [2].
- **Many trials from few groups.** In our database, 253 of 411 human studies (62%) come from Japan, China or Taiwan, and a single researcher is an author on 34 of them, including four trials cited in this guide [5][10][19][21]. That isn't a sign of misconduct, but it means fewer independent replications.
- **Animal results aren't human results.** Hydrogen's most dramatic reported effects, such as on stroke damage, come from rodents and cells [7].

For how to judge hydrogen claims yourself, see [is hydrogen water real or fake?](/blog/hydrogen-water-real-or-fake), or browse studies by outcome in our [benefits explorer](/explore-by-benefit).

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the proven benefits of hydrogen water?

None is proven in the medical sense. The most consistent findings are slightly lower perceived exercise effort and blood lactate in healthy adults, and a small drop in total and LDL cholesterol in adults with overweight or obesity. Claims about sleep, mood, skin, brain health and disease rest on small trials or animal studies.

### Is molecular hydrogen good for you?

It may have small effects on exercise fatigue and cholesterol, and drinking hydrogen water appears low-risk for most healthy adults. But evidence for broader benefits is weak, and the largest trials in patients found no benefit over placebo.

### What is molecular hydrogen therapy?

It's the experimental use of hydrogen gas (H₂) for health, usually by drinking water with dissolved hydrogen or inhaling a hydrogen gas mixture. Most human studies are small and short. No disease treatment has been established, and larger trials in Parkinson's disease and COVID-19 found no benefit.

### Does hydrogen water improve exercise performance?

Not for most measures. Pooled trials found no improvement in endurance, VO2max or strength, and only a small gain in jumping power. Hydrogen did make exercise feel slightly easier and lowered blood lactate a little.

### Is hydrogen water better than regular water?

In most trials the comparison was ordinary water, and differences were small or absent. Hydrogen water slightly outperformed plain water on exercise effort and cholesterol, but not on endurance, insulin resistance, memory or disease outcomes.
