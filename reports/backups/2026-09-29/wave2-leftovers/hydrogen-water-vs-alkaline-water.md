**Short answer:** Hydrogen water and alkaline water are defined by different things. Hydrogen water contains dissolved hydrogen gas (H2). Alkaline water has a pH above 7. They overlap in one product: water ionizers, which make "ionized" water (electrolyzed reduced water) that is alkaline and holds some hydrogen, usually 0.2 to 1.2 mg/L when the machine is new and clean [1]. When researchers tested the two properties separately in animals, the hydrogen, not the high pH, explained the effects [2]. Human evidence for alkaline water's own health claims is thin [3], and hydrogen water's effects in people have been small [4][5]. Ionizer water above pH 9.8 isn't recommended, and people with kidney disease should check with their doctor first [1].

## How do hydrogen water and alkaline water compare?

| | Hydrogen water | Alkaline water | Ionizer water ("ionized" or electrolyzed reduced water) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defined by | Dissolved hydrogen gas (H2) | A pH above 7 | Made by electrolysis; alkaline and contains some H2 [2] |
| pH | Depends on how it's made; the hydrogen itself doesn't change pH [2] | Above 7; for example, pH 8.8 and 9.1 in two studies below [6][7] | About 8.5 to 10.5 at common settings, sometimes higher [1] |
| Hydrogen content | About 1.6 mg/L when saturated at normal pressure; more only under pressure or while sealed [2] | None, unless it's also made by electrolysis | Usually 0.2–1.2 mg/L when new; under 0.01 mg/L once electrodes scale up [1] |
| How it's made | Magnesium tablets, electrolysis bottles, pressurized bottles, sealed pouches | Natural or added minerals such as bicarbonate, or electrolysis | Household ionizer connected to tap water [1] |
| What studies credit for any effect | The dissolved hydrogen [2] | Mostly the minerals, especially bicarbonate, not the pH number [2][8] | The hydrogen, when pH and hydrogen were tested separately in animals [2] |
| Main safety notes | Few, mild side effects in trials; tablets add magnesium | Check sodium and other minerals on the label | Don't drink it above pH 9.8; kidney disease needs medical supervision [1] |

## Is hydrogen water the same as alkaline water?

No. Hydrogen water is ordinary water with hydrogen gas dissolved in it. The gas doesn't make water alkaline: dissolved H2 doesn't break apart into ions, whatever the pH [2]. Alkaline water is any water with a pH above 7, usually from dissolved minerals or from electrolysis. Plain alkaline water has no added hydrogen.

People mix them up because water ionizers make water that is both, and for decades the high pH got the credit. Lab and animal studies later pulled the two apart [2]:

- In rats with aspirin-induced stomach injury, ionizer water at pH 10.5 was protective, but water brought to the same pH with sodium hydroxide (lye) wasn't.
- In a similar rat study, ionizer water with 0.22 or 0.84 mg/L of hydrogen was protective, while 0.07 mg/L wasn't. At the same hydrogen level, pH 8.5 worked as well as pH 9.5.
- In several lab and animal experiments, removing the hydrogen from ionizer water removed its effects.

That's why reviewers concluded that hydrogen, not alkalinity, explains the reported effects of ionizer water [2]. These are animal and lab results. We found no human trial that compared hydrogen water with alkaline water head to head. For the basics, see [what is hydrogen water?](/blog/what-is-hydrogen-water); for a comparison with tap water, see [hydrogen water vs regular water](/blog/hydrogen-water-vs-regular-water).

## What is ionized hydrogen water?

"Ionized hydrogen water," "alkaline ionized water" and "alkaline reduced water" are other names for electrolyzed reduced water, the water that comes out of a water ionizer [2]. Ionizers are sold under brand names such as Kangen (by Enagic) and Tyent. Japan's government approved water ionizers in 1965 to relieve digestive complaints, and Korea followed in 1978 [2].

**How an ionizer works.** An electric current runs through tap water between two electrodes separated by a membrane. At the negative electrode (the cathode), water splits into hydrogen gas and hydroxide ions, so that stream comes out alkaline with some dissolved hydrogen. That's the water you drink. At the positive electrode (the anode), the water turns acidic, and if it contains chloride, chlorine compounds form there too, which is why the acidic stream is used for cleaning [1].

"Ionized" is a misnomer. The water molecules themselves aren't ions, and the finished water doesn't hold more ions overall than the tap water it came from [2].

**How much hydrogen does ionizer water contain?** Less than many buyers expect, and it varies a lot [1]:

- Most ionizers, when new and fed suitable tap water at a normal flow, make about 0.2–1.2 mg/L of hydrogen at pH 8.5–10.5. The full range runs from under 0.01 to nearly 2 mg/L.
- The tap water needs at least about 50 mg/L of minerals. In areas with very soft water, an ionizer may add little hydrogen.
- In some machines the reviewers tested, mineral scale on the electrodes cut output from nearly 1 mg/L to under 0.01 mg/L within two weeks; cleaning with citric acid restored it.
- The pH buttons on most ionizers change the voltage, not a measured pH, so the water can come out above pH 10 even on a "9.5" setting.

**The pH–hydrogen trade-off.** Making hydrogen at the cathode also makes hydroxide, so getting more hydrogen out of an ionizer, for example by slowing the flow, also pushes the pH up. Some ionizers reach about 0.7 mg/L before the pH passes 10; many can't [1]. For comparison, water saturated with hydrogen at normal pressure holds about 1.6 mg/L [2]. Our [hydrogen water ppm guide](/blog/hydrogen-water-ppm-levels) explains what these numbers mean.

## Do ORP readings or "microclusters" show which water is better?

No. Two popular sales claims don't hold up.

**ORP (oxidation-reduction potential).** Sellers often show a strongly negative ORP reading as proof of antioxidant power. In ionizer water the negative reading does come from dissolved hydrogen, but pH moves it far more [1]. At pH 9, water with 0.1 mg/L of hydrogen reads about −497 mV. Ten times more hydrogen (1 mg/L) only moves it to about −527 mV, within a typical meter's error, and raising the pH by half a unit moves it just as much. Water at pH 10.5 can read −580 to −730 mV while holding less than 0.1 mg/L of hydrogen [1]. So neither ORP meters nor "hydrogen meters" that calculate from ORP can tell you how much hydrogen is in the water [1].

**"Microclustered" water.** The idea that ionizer water forms smaller, more hydrating clusters of water molecules came from a lab measurement (NMR) that turned out to shift with pH, not with water structure. The popular demonstration of ionizer water "brewing" tea without heat works because some tea compounds change color with pH [2].

## Does alkaline water change your body's pH?

Not much, and ionizer water especially can't. A water's ability to neutralize acid, called buffering, comes from dissolved minerals such as bicarbonate, not from its pH number [2]. Your body keeps blood pH in a narrow range using blood buffers, breathing and the kidneys [7].

**Our calculation.** How much acid a liter of each water can neutralize, and how many liters it would take to match one teaspoon (4.8 g) of baking soda, which neutralizes about 57 millimoles of acid:

| Water | pH | Acid it can neutralize, per liter | Liters to match 1 tsp of baking soda |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionizer water (extra hydroxide only) | 9.5 | ≈0.03 mmol | ≈1,800 |
| Ionizer water (extra hydroxide only) | 10.0 | ≈0.1 mmol | ≈570 |
| Bicarbonate mineral water from an athlete study [7] | 9.1 | ≈11 mmol | ≈5 |
| Bicarbonate-rich mineral water from a bone study [8] | Not reported | ≈36 mmol | ≈1.6 |

*Ionizer rows count only the hydroxide that raises the pH (at 25 °C); the source tap water adds a little buffering of its own. Mineral-water rows use the bicarbonate and carbonate levels the studies reported. Reviewers reached the same 1,800-liter figure for pH 9.5 water [2].*

The takeaway: a label's pH tells you little. Waters rich in bicarbonate raised urine pH in short studies [7][8], while high-pH ionizer water carries almost no buffering, so reviewers concluded it won't shift the body's pH any more than the tap water it came from [2].

## What does the research show for alkaline water?

Not much that's specific to a high pH. The studies usually cited fall into four groups:

- **Reflux (lab only).** A 2012 lab study found that a naturally bicarbonate-rich well water at pH 8.8 permanently inactivated human pepsin, a digestive enzyme involved in reflux damage, in test tubes. It also buffered acid better than two ordinary bottled waters [6]. Nobody drank the water in this study.
- **Bone markers.** In a 4-week randomized study of 30 young women, a mineral water very high in bicarbonate lowered parathyroid hormone and a blood marker of bone breakdown, while a calcium-rich water low in bicarbonate didn't [8]. That's a marker measured over weeks, and the authors credited the bicarbonate.
- **Hydration and exercise.** In 100 healthy adults dehydrated by exercise, a commercial electrolyzed high-pH water lowered one measure of blood thickness (high-shear viscosity) more than standard bottled water, 6.3% vs 3.4%. Other hydration markers didn't differ [9]. In 16 combat-sport athletes, 3 weeks of a bicarbonate mineral water at pH 9.1 raised urine and resting blood pH and improved 30-second sprint-test results compared with baseline, while ordinary table water didn't [7].
- **Cancer.** A systematic review found no randomized trials of alkaline water or an alkaline diet for preventing or treating cancer, and concluded that promoting alkaline water to the public for cancer isn't justified [3].

Human trials of ionizer water are small and mixed, and many didn't report the water's hydrogen content [1]. In 27 adults with diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome, 8 weeks of ionizer water (pH 8.5–10) didn't beat placebo water on the main symptom outcome, though a quality-of-life score improved more; hydrogen wasn't reported [10]. In 50 adults with type 2 diabetes, electrolyzed hydrogen-rich water didn't change insulin resistance, the main outcome, compared with filtered water [11].

## What does the research show for hydrogen water?

Small effects, on a limited set of outcomes. Pooled trials in 597 healthy adults found that hydrogen slightly lowered how hard exercise felt and blood lactate, but didn't improve endurance or strength [4]. A 2026 meta-analysis of 13 trials in 757 adults with overweight or obesity found total cholesterol about 6.7 mg/dL lower and LDL about 3.2 mg/dL lower, changes the authors called too small to matter for heart risk [5]. No disease treatment has been established. ## Is alkaline water or hydrogen water safe?

For most healthy people, both appear low-risk at ordinary strengths. The concerns are specific.

**High-pH ionizer water.** Rules in Japan and Korea say ionizer drinking water shouldn't exceed pH 9.8, and reviewers recommend following that limit [1]. Their reasons:

- Some people with poor kidney function developed high blood potassium (hyperkalemia) after drinking ionizer water above pH 10. The cause isn't known; metal leaching from worn electrodes is one suspect [1].
- In 1990s animal studies, rats drinking ionizer water developed heart-tissue damage and high potassium; rats given pH 11.2–12 water for a year grew poorly [1].
- Electrodes are usually platinum-coated titanium. At high settings, with mineral-rich water and slow flow, tiny amounts of platinum and other metals may shed into the water [1].
- Less-studied concerns include reduced absorption of some nutrients, gut bacterial overgrowth and irritation of the gut lining [1].

People with impaired kidney function shouldn't use ionizer water without medical supervision, the reviewers advise [1]; see [is hydrogen water good for your kidneys?](/blog/is-hydrogen-water-good-for-kidneys)

**Bottled alkaline water.** Look at the minerals, not just the pH, especially if you've been told to limit sodium. The alkaline water in one athlete study contained about 255 mg of sodium per liter [7].

**Hydrogen water.** Human trials have reported few side effects, mostly mild and digestive; see [hydrogen water side effects](/blog/hydrogen-water-side-effects). Magnesium tablets add magnesium to every glass, which matters if you have kidney disease; see [hydrogen tablets: side effects and magnesium](/blog/h2-tabs-side-effects).

## Which is easier to use day to day?

If you want hydrogen, tablets, portable electrolysis bottles with a membrane and ready-to-drink hydrogen water skip the ionizer's pH trade-off and its dependence on tap-water minerals [1]. Ionizers are the highest-maintenance route: they need suitable tap water, regular descaling and a hydrogen-specific test, not an ORP meter, to know what you're drinking [1]. If you just like the taste of alkaline or mineral water, bottled water is simplest; compare bicarbonate and sodium on the label, not just pH. More on concentration: [ppm levels](/blog/hydrogen-water-ppm-levels) and [how much hydrogen water per day](/blog/how-much-hydrogen-water-per-day).

## What the evidence doesn't show

- **No head-to-head human trials.** We found no human trial comparing hydrogen water directly with alkaline water. Trials of ionizer water compared it with plain or filtered water, which can't separate the effect of hydrogen from the effect of pH [10][11].
- **The "hydrogen, not pH" answer comes from animals.** The experiments that credited hydrogen were done in rats and in lab dishes [2].
- **Alkaline water's human studies measure markers.** The bone, hydration and acid-base results are short-term changes in blood or urine markers, often from bicarbonate-rich mineral waters rather than high pH alone [7][8][9].
- **Many ionizer studies didn't measure hydrogen.** Reviewers estimate that perhaps more than half of ionizer-water studies don't address the hydrogen at all [1], so the dose people got is often unknown.
- **Long-term safety of high-pH ionizer water is unsettled.** The high-potassium reports and animal findings haven't been explained, and reviewers call for more safety research [1].
- **Hydrogen water's benefits are small and short-term so far** [4][5].

## Frequently asked questions

### Is hydrogen water better than alkaline water?

Neither has strong evidence. In animal studies, the effects of ionizer water came from its hydrogen, not its pH. In people, hydrogen water's effects have been small, and alkaline water's studies mostly measured short-term blood or urine markers. No trial has compared the two directly.

### Is ionized water the same as hydrogen water?

Not exactly. Ionized water, also called electrolyzed reduced water, is alkaline water from a water ionizer that also contains some dissolved hydrogen, typically 0.2–1.2 mg/L when the machine is new and clean. Hydrogen water simply means water with dissolved hydrogen, at any pH.

### Is Kangen water hydrogen water?

Kangen water is the brand name for water from Enagic's ionizers, so it's electrolyzed reduced water: alkaline, with some dissolved hydrogen. The amount depends on the setting, the minerals in your tap water, the flow rate and how clean the electrodes are. We don't test or rate brands; only a hydrogen-specific test shows what's in the glass.

### Is hydrogen water alkaline?

Not necessarily. Dissolved hydrogen doesn't change pH. Water from ionizers and magnesium-based products is alkaline, while portable electrolysis bottles with a membrane and hydrogen dissolved under pressure can be close to neutral.

### Is alkaline water safe if you have kidney disease?

Ask your nephrologist first. High-pH ionizer water has been linked to high blood potassium in some people with poor kidney function, and reviewers advise against using it without medical supervision. Bottled alkaline waters can also add sodium or other minerals you may need to limit.
